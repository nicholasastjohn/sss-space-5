# Source Policy

Version 0.1 — 2026-09-01

## Principle

Every material claim that affects eligibility, a category score, or rank must be traceable to a dated public source. Source quality matters more than source volume.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the highest available tier.

### Tier 1 — Primary authoritative sources

- SEC filings and equivalent securities-regulator filings.
- Audited financial statements and official earnings releases.
- Government contract and award records, budget documents, and agency releases.
- Official mission records, launch manifests, licensing records, and technical documentation.
- Court, regulator, or exchange records when legally relevant.

### Tier 2 — Company primary sources

- Investor presentations, earnings calls, product documentation, and official press releases.
- Treat forward-looking claims, non-GAAP measures, total-addressable-market estimates, and contract headline values as claims requiring context.

### Tier 3 — High-quality secondary sources

- Reputable financial, trade, scientific, or technical reporting with identifiable authorship and direct sourcing.
- Use primarily for context or corroboration when a primary record is unavailable.

### Tier 4 — Discovery only

- Aggregators, unsourced databases, search snippets, social posts, forums, newsletters, and AI-generated summaries.
- These may identify a lead but cannot independently support a score-changing claim.

## Citation requirements

For each material claim, record:

- direct URL;
- publisher or issuing body;
- publication or filing date;
- relevant reporting period;
- access date when the page may change; and
- whether the figure is reported, calculated, estimated, or inferred.

Link to the underlying filing or record, not a search-results page. Quote sparingly and preserve the source’s meaning.

## Freshness rules

- Financial condition: use the most recent filed reporting period available by the cutoff.
- Backlog and guidance: use the latest official disclosure and retain its definition.
- Operational milestones: verify the event date, not just the article date.
- Static technical facts may use older sources when still authoritative; document why they remain current.
- If a material source postdates the cutoff, defer it to the next weekly ranking.

## Reconciliation rules

When sources conflict:

1. Prefer the more authoritative and more recent source when periods and definitions match.
2. Check whether the sources use different units, scopes, currencies, contract definitions, or reporting periods.
3. State the conflict explicitly when it cannot be resolved.
4. Lower confidence or mark the company ineligible if the conflict could materially change rank.

## Prohibited inputs

Do not use nonpublic, employer-confidential, customer-confidential, classified, controlled, proprietary, export-controlled, procurement-sensitive, or material nonpublic information. Do not ask a contributor to obtain or confirm such information. Exclude uncertain information.
